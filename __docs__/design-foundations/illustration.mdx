# Illustration

HSK AI illustrations use a consistent, purposeful visual language across the learner PWA and Android app. Ordinary supporting illustrations are restrained, minimal, flat and geometric. The established HSK AI Panda, other approved characters and the red pearlescent AI Voice Orb retain their canonical appearance instead of being redrawn to fit a generic flat style.

## Audience calibration

The same system serves multiple audiences. The principles don't change — the emotional register does.

### Learner side

Friendly and recognizable, without turning every screen into decoration. Use the established HSK AI Panda or another approved character where it helps guide, explain or celebrate learning. Preserve its canonical identity across states. In ordinary lessons, preserve existing character reactions and visual effects for BOTH correct and incorrect answers: a brief positive reaction to a correct response and a clear, supportive reaction to an incorrect response. Such immediate feedback is part of teaching, not unnecessary decoration; do not remove it under the flat-illustration or minimal-motion rules. Keep Hanzi, pinyin (when appropriate), the actual question, the answer state, and the next action readable. Reserve major confetti/celebration sequences for genuine milestones rather than every correct tap.

### Settings and utility screens

Keep illustrations sparse, minimal and supportive. Prefer concise text and clear controls on settings, reports and utility screens. Character art is contextual, not mandatory decoration; use a more restrained emotional register than in learner celebrations.

## Usage guidelines

Guidance for how to apply illustration correctly across contexts. Visual examples will be defined here as the system matures.

### Contrast by meaning

Ask first: if this illustration were removed, would the user lose meaning? If yes, it's meaningful — apply 3:1.
Any meaningful Panda or approved-character feature used to communicate identity or an actionable state must be distinguishable from its background; apply 3:1 contrast to meaningful graphical elements. Decorative accessories are not required to meet that graphical contrast threshold.
Confetti and accent shapes are always decorative — no hard minimum, but avoid combinations that feel unintentional.

### Flat and geometric — ordinary illustrations

Use flat, minimal, geometric forms for ordinary supporting illustrations. Avoid unnecessary gradients, drop shadows, glow or dimensional shading.
Corners and edges should feel intentional — rounded only where the form calls for it.
Complexity should come from composition, not from surface treatment.
Do not apply this flat-style rule to canonical Panda/approved character artwork or to the red pearlescent AI Voice Orb. The Orb retains its approved 3D look, glow and purposeful animated Listening, Thinking and Speaking states.

### Color from Graphics tokens

Use approved HSK AI graphics/illustration tokens for new ordinary illustrations. Preserve the existing canonical colors of the Panda and other approved characters; do not substitute an unrelated lookalike or invent a new palette. Where a token mapping does not yet exist, use the established source artwork as the reference rather than guessing a hex value.
Keep learning moments engaging but reduce saturation and illustration density in settings and utility contexts.
Keep the AI Voice Orb's approved red pearlescent appearance and expressive motion independent of the flat illustration palette.

## Types of illustration

Each tier has a distinct purpose, audience, and contrast requirement. Identifying the correct tier is the first step in any illustration decision.

### HSK AI Panda and approved characters

The existing Panda is the canonical Panda on every screen and platform. Preserve its recognizable face, eyes, ears, body proportions, silhouette, colors, accessories and illustration style. Only pose, gaze, gesture, expression, movement or scene may vary, without changing identity. The same rule applies to each other approved HSK AI character. Reuse approved source artwork and renderers; never replace a character with a merely similar AI-generated mascot. Lesson characters MUST retain their established correct-answer and incorrect-answer expressions, gestures and related visual effects on both PWA and Android; do not flatten, hide, disable, or replace these reactions when applying design-system consistency. Ensure feedback is respectful and never shames learners for errors.

### AI Voice Orb

The existing red pearlescent Orb is a specialized interactive visual, not an ordinary flat illustration. Preserve its recognizable red, three-dimensional, softly glowing form and its approved Listening, Thinking and Speaking motions. Its movement must remain purposeful, not obstruct the conversation or learning task, and respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Spot illustration

Error states, empty states, and contextual illustrations composed of shapes to convey meaning in moments where data or content is absent. Yet, they are always paired with text that carries the same meaning, making them decorative — no contrast minimum, though mindfulness is always encouraged.

### Scene

Supporting scenes and learning environments use simple, consistent shapes and intentional composition. Do not introduce a different Panda or unapproved character identity into a scene. Assess each meaningful element individually for contrast requirements.

<img src={typesOfIllustration4Img} alt="Types of illustration 4" />

### Accent

Floating shapes, confetti blasts, and celebration scatter elements used purely for delight and energy. Always decorative — no contrast minimum, though mindfulness is always encouraged.

## Accessibility

The key question: if this illustration were removed, would the user lose meaning? If yes — apply 3:1.

| Type | Meaningful | Decorative | Contextual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contrast requirement | 3:1 | No requirement | Assess |
| Rationale | The illustration communicates something the learner must understand. Examples: a meaningful character status symbol or an essential lesson diagram. | The illustration adds delight but carries no meaning the learner must interpret. No hard minimum — mindfulness is still encouraged. Examples: decorative Panda accessories, confetti and floating accent shapes. | Partly meaningful, partly decorative — assess each element individually. Example: in a lesson scene, a necessary direction marker is meaningful (3:1 required), while a decorative accessory is not. |

## Roles and responsibilities

Illustration is a craft and a product decision simultaneously — where it appears and what it looks like are inseparable. Visual designers own both.

| Responsibility | Visual design | Product design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Create all illustration assets | Owns | – |
| Determine whether a moment needs illustration | Owns | – |
| Decide which tier and emotional register is appropriate | Owns | – |
| Ensure new illustration fits the existing system | Owns | – |
| Flag moments that might benefit from illustration | Contributes | Contributes |
