# Modals

<NeutralBadge label="Focused task or decision" />

A high-focus overlay used to isolate the learner's attention for a specific sub-task or important decision. A modal requires explicit action or dismissal before returning to background content. In HSK AI, the presentation adapts to iPhone PWA and native Android, retaining existing approved mobile sheets and centered dialogs instead of copying Khan Academy's desktop geometry.

## <Do /> When to use

The choice to use a modal is determined by the need for complete user focus and the "sandboxing" of a specific workflow.

| Use when | Example use |
| --- | --- |
| A high-stakes decision requires the user’s full attention | Leaving an unfinished HSK lesson or deleting an account. |
| The task is important but can stay within the current context | Changing a study option or choosing an available HSK level. |
| The system requires an explicit "Confirm" or "Cancel" before proceeding | Restarting an exercise or confirming a deletion. |
| A short sequential flow is needed without leaving the page | Choosing an option and reviewing it before confirmation, if the existing HSK AI flow requires this. |
| Supplementary content is needed without fully navigating away from the current page | Viewing contextual help for a Chinese learning exercise. |

## <Dont /> When NOT to use

Avoid modals when the task doesn’t require the user’s full attention, when the content is too complex to contain, or when a lighter in-context interaction would be less disruptive.

| Don’t use when | Recommended alternative | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The learner needs to monitor or interact with the underlying lesson or AI Voice chat | In-context explanation or a non-modal panel | Do not block the lesson, character reactions or voice interaction for information that can coexist with the main task. |
| The content is dense or complex enough to need its own page | Full-screen flow | Long forms, lengthy lessons and complex multi-step tasks belong in a dedicated view, especially on mobile. |
| The interaction is a simple, non-destructive setting change | Inline control or compact popover | Do not interrupt study with a modal when an immediate local control is sufficient. High-risk binary choices still require a confirmation. |
| A modal is already open | Redesign the flow | Never stack task modals. A second modal that introduces new content or choices disorients the user and breaks their sense of where they are. The one exception is a confirmation that resolves the dismissal of the modal beneath it — see Dismissal and unsaved state. |

## Variants

Modals are a universal container, but their internal logic changes based on the user’s goal. The following sections detail the modal flavors used across the product.

### Confirmation

Use for destructive or non-reversible actions.


### Functional task

Use for focused HSK AI actions such as configuring a practice option, confirming an account change or selecting a supported learning preference.

Multi-step flows have additional requirements — see Multi-step flows under Interaction.


### Selection (configuration)

Use for complex, multi-faceted filtering or export settings.

See <a href="./?path=/docs/design-guidelines-components-overlays-modals-selection--docs">Modals - selection</a>.


### Contextual education

Triggered by info icons. Keeps the explanation local to the context.


### Marketing and announcement

Reserve for an already-approved HSK AI feature introduction when interruption is justified. Do not reproduce Khan Academy reward, character or accessory marketing. Pro/payment information belongs in the product's established subscription flow, not an unsolicited modal that hides the lesson.


### Media

Use for viewing an image or learning resource, normally read-only. Do not make interactive Hanzi exercises read-only by applying this variant.


## Usage and purpose by variant

| Variant | Purpose | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmation | To verify a high-stakes or permanent action and prevent errors. | Deleting an account or leaving a lesson with unsaved progress. |
| Functional task | To complete a short task or edit without leaving the current context. | Choosing a lesson setting or changing a study preference. |
| Selection (configuration) | To narrow or configure a view with multiple options. | Selecting available HSK levels, exercise types or progress filters. |
| Contextual education | To explain a specific term or learning feature without losing context. | Explaining a grammar hint, vocabulary detail or mastery indicator. |
| Marketing and announcement | To introduce a relevant approved capability without interrupting active study. | A context-sensitive explanation of a genuinely new HSK AI feature, when permitted by the product's existing announcement rules. |
| Media | To view media and its context. | Expanding a lesson image or reference image. |

## Sizing reference

Do not copy fixed Khan Academy desktop modal widths into HSK AI's phone experience. Choose the container from the task, the available viewport and existing platform patterns; the *intent* of the original sizing rule (clear focus and readable density) remains authoritative.

| Variant | HSK AI mobile presentation | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmation | A compact centered dialog where possible, fitting within the safe viewport. | Keeps the consequence and two decisions together without occupying the whole screen. |
| Contextual education | Compact dialog or a short bottom sheet, following the existing screen pattern. | Explains a topic near the context without obscuring the entire lesson. |
| Functional task | Bottom sheet for a short task; dedicated view for long forms and extensive keyboard input. | Supports mobile interaction, accessibility and predictable keyboard handling. |
| Selection (configuration) | Bottom sheet with a scrollable content area and reachable actions. | Makes several choices usable without importing a desktop multi-column form. |
| Marketing and announcement | Use only an approved HSK AI announcement layout and cadence. | Prevents unrelated promotional takeovers of the learning screen. |
| Media | Responsive full-screen or appropriately fitted viewer. | Keeps media legible and avoids clipping. |

For larger screens, the dialog may use an appropriate maximum width; do not stretch it simply because space exists. For iPhone PWA respect browser viewport changes and safe-area insets; on Android respect window insets and system navigation.

## Surface and elevation

A true modal must be visually separate from and block interaction with the background. Use HSK AI's approved raised-surface, overlay and elevation tokens; a scrim (dimmed background) must cover and deactivate underlying content. Full-screen media or dedicated screens are not floating dialogs. Preserve established HSK AI glass effects only where already approved; do not force them onto every modal. See <a href="./?path=/docs/design-guidelines-foundations-surfaces-and-elevations--docs">Surfaces and elevations</a>.

| Element | Value | Token |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Modal container | Approved HSK AI raised surface | PWA: existing surface tokens; Android: existing surface theme |
| Container separation | Enough elevation or boundary to distinguish the dialog | Reuse approved HSK AI platform components |
| Background | Modal scrim | Reuse the current modal overlay with sufficient separation and contrast |

## Placement

### Adaptive placement

Use a centered dialog for brief confirmations; use an existing bottom sheet for mobile selection or short configuration tasks; use a dedicated page or full-screen flow for long interactions. The overlay must cover and deactivate all background content, including the existing bottom navigation. A bottom sheet is not a desktop dialog shrunk to fit: it must respect safe areas, keyboard clearance, scrolling, and tap targets.


### Actions always reachable

On phones, keep the primary action visible at the sheet/dialog footer where applicable; a single primary action may span the available width. Grouped actions may be side by side when readable or vertically stacked in narrow viewports or with larger text. Preserve the approved primary/secondary hierarchy and logical reading order for the selected language, rather than enforcing desktop bottom-right alignment. Read-only media viewers do not need footer actions.


### Overflow

A floating dialog or bottom sheet must fit within the usable visual viewport after accounting for system bars, the home indicator, the safe area and an opened keyboard. When content exceeds available height, scroll the body rather than losing the title or required actions. Preserve a usable close/escape route and prevent the background page from scrolling while the modal is active. Do not cover the AI Voice controls, lesson answers or account payment confirmation with an unrelated overlay.

## Interaction

### Focus

Opening a modal moves focus into it — to the first interactive element, or to the modal itself when the content is read-only. While the modal is open, focus is trapped inside it: tabbing past the last element returns to the first, and background content is unreachable by keyboard.

Closing the modal returns focus to the element that opened it, so a keyboard user resumes where they left off rather than at the top of the page.

The modal is exposed to assistive technology as a dialog, labelled by its title, with background content hidden.

### Dismissal and unsaved state

Provide a clearly available close/back path, including platform back behavior where applicable. Outside-tap dismissal is suitable only for safe, non-destructive cases; do not accidentally close high-stakes payment, account or loss-of-progress decisions. For task modals with unsaved changes, confirm before discarding user input. In HSK AI's active lessons, leaving or restarting must respect the existing progress-protection behavior.

This confirmation is the only permitted second layer. It resolves the dismissal of the modal beneath it and nothing else — it introduces no new task, offers no route to a third layer, and on resolution either returns the user to the modal they were in or closes both.


### Multi-step flows

A modal can support a short sequential flow of 2–3 steps when each step is simple and self-contained. If the flow grows beyond 3 steps or requires significant input at each stage, move it to a full page with the <a href="./?path=/docs/design-guidelines-components-feedback-progress-stepper--docs">Progress stepper</a> instead.


## Buttons

### Pairings

Modal actions follow the established HSK AI <a href="./?path=/docs/design-guidelines-components-buttons-buttons--docs">button hierarchy</a>, without importing Khan-specific learner button styling. Three pairings apply:

-   **Standard (Primary + Secondary):** for ordinary HSK AI functional tasks (e.g., "Save settings"). The secondary option provides an understandable exit.
-   **Nudge (Primary + Tertiary):** used for confirmations. The primary solid button represents the "path of least resistance." Whether this is the safe choice or the requested action depends on the risk involved (see Directional logic).
-   **Destructive (Critical + Tertiary):** for permanent or data-losing actions such as account deletion or discarding unfinished work. Use the distinct destructive/error color rather than treating HSK AI's standard brand-red action as destructive. Prefer a safe action as primary when loss of progress is at risk.


### Directional logic

-   **High-risk nudges:** when leaving a lesson, resetting progress or discarding unsaved work, emphasize the safe choice (e.g., "Continue studying") and make the loss explicit. This prevents accidental progress loss.
-   **Standard intent:** when an action is neutral and expected (e.g., "Log out"), the requested action may remain primary, provided no unsaved work, payment verification or other important process is at risk.


## Copy

### Headline titles

Modal titles should be direct and specific — ideally a noun or short noun phrase that describes exactly what the modal is for. Avoid generic titles like "Are you sure?" or "Notice."


### Describing body content

The body copy should explain the consequence of the action clearly and concisely. For destructive actions, be explicit about what will be lost. Never rely on the title alone to carry the full context.


### Action labels

Use explicit verbs that describe exactly what will happen. The user should be able to read the button label alone and understand the outcome. Avoid generic labels that require reading the full modal to understand.
